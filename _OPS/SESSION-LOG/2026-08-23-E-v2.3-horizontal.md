## The two questions, answered honestly

**Is RECON necessary?** — **Not as scoped.** Two of its five questions stopped mattering, one changed owner, and two became *more* important. Replaced with a 3–5 day, mostly-remote Evidence Sprint across two verticals. The one part that cannot be skipped: **100–200 real documents**. Building an extraction pipeline against imagined documents is the most expensive available mistake, and it is vertical-independent.

**Can AI build the question bank?** — **Yes for drafting, no for authorising.** The binding constraint is **licensing, not capability**. And the pipeline turned out to be more valuable than expected: it *productises* CUSTOMISE, which is what makes the horizontal thesis executable at all.

## The thing I pushed back on

The horizontal positioning is strong — but it is **only protective if the architecture is genuinely horizontal.** If healthcare ships clinical rules, a medical question bank and clinical urgency language while legal ships none of it, healthcare is a different product wearing the same name, and a regulator will look at healthcare. That produced ADR-031's three binding rules and OT-15 (the vertical pack refactor).

## The observation I raised

**Healthcare is the hardest vertical to start in** — handwritten documents, mixed-literacy one-time users, device classification, health-data rules, 25-year retention, clinical sign-off, price-sensitive slow-cycle buyers. Legal/accounting/recruitment have none of that. Not an argument to abandon healthcare; an argument to *decide* rather than drift. Recorded as OT-17, decided by the Evidence Sprint.

Counter-argument recorded too: an engine hardened on handwritten prescriptions handles legal PDFs trivially. The reverse is not true.
